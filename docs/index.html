Exemplar Methods from Scratch: kNN, KDE, Metric Learning and Open-Set Face Matching

How it was built

Part 1: K Nearest Neighbours and the Factor of 2

Build the probabilistic KNN classifier of PML 16.1 with a Mahalanobis metric, reproduce the U-shaped test curve of Figure 16.2 on the book's own data, and put the claim that 1-NN gets within a factor of 2 of the Bayes error to a measured test.

Part 2: The Curse of Dimensionality

Measure the two faces of the curse from PML 16.1.2: how large a cube has to get before it holds a small fraction of uniform data, and how fast KNN accuracy collapses when you add dimensions that carry no signal.

Part 3: Kernel Density Estimation

Turn exemplars into a density with the Parzen window estimator of PML 16.3: the four kernels of Table 16.1, Silverman's rule with a robust sigma, a log-sum-exp safe density in any dimension, and bandwidth selection by leave-one-out likelihood. Then test the rule of thumb on data that is not Gaussian.

Part 4: From KDE Back to KNN

Close the loop of PML 16.3.4: derive KNN as a generative classifier whose class conditionals are balloon density estimates, verify the cancellation in eq 16.35 to machine precision, and find out what the balloon estimate gives up. Then compare a fixed-bandwidth KDE classifier with KNN on the data of Figure 16.2.

Part 5: Kernel Regression

Use a KDE of the joint to predict y from x (PML 16.3.5). Build the Nadaraya-Watson mean and variance, choose the bandwidth by leave-one-out on the data of Figure 16.10, check whether eq 16.45 gives honest error bars, and fix the boundary bias with locally linear regression.

Part 6: Learning a Mahalanobis Metric

Stop assuming every dimension matters equally. Implement neighbourhood components analysis and large margin nearest neighbours (PML 16.2.1) with hand-derived gradients that share one matrix identity, optimise them, and see how much of the accuracy lost to noise dimensions comes back.

Part 7: Faces and Ranking Losses

Move to real data: Olivetti faces, split by person. Build the ranking losses of PML 16.2.4 with exact gradients on the unit sphere (contrastive, triplet with hard and semi-hard mining, N-pairs and the centroid upper bound), and train a linear embedding on class-balanced batches.

Part 8: Open-Set Face Matching

Build the system PML 16.1.4 describes: a gallery of known people, a similarity threshold, and the answer 'I have not seen this person'. Tune the threshold on people the metric never saw, compare every metric in a fair paired test over many splits, and let the gallery grow online.

Part 9: Searching Faster

Face the running-time problem of PML 16.1.3. Build a k-d tree that finds exact neighbours by pruning, measure where the pruning stops working as dimension grows, and then give up exactness with locally sensitive hashing.
